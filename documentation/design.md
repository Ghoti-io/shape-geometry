# Design

**Status:** parts 1 to 4 of the shape-geometry plan: curves, model paths,
bounds and the hash spelling; maps, the orientation rules, the exact
predicates and the route predicate; the 26.6 device path and the integer
flattener (revision 2); along-path parametrization; the fill rules; strokes:
the definition of the covered region and the expansion (construction revision
1), solid and dashed (SG19 to SG30), and a DXF simple linetype lowered to a dash
array.

Paths, transforms and flattening for the shape family, bit-identical on every host.

The dependency rules and the determinism gates that bind this library are in
the Makefile's header comment and in `tools/`: `check-fp-contract`,
`check-edges`, `check-libm` and `check-no-x87`, each shown to fail on a planted
violation by `make check-gates`.

## Model

Model space is `double`, y down. A curve (`curve.h`) is a value: a kind (line,
quadratic, cubic, rational quadratic, rational cubic), a dimension (2 or 3),
control points and, for the rational kinds, a positive weight at each. A path
(`path.h`) is the opaque builder: `move_to`, the drawing verbs, `close`.

Every number is checked when it enters: NaN and infinity are `ERR_INVALID`, a
finite number beyond 2^100 (or a weight outside 2^-100 .. 2^100) is
`ERR_LIMIT`. That bound is what lets every evaluation and every bound be
finite without a check at the end. A failed call leaves a path unchanged.

## Bounds

The box of a curve is the box of the curve, not of its control polygon. For
each coordinate, written `N/D` (`D = 1` for a polynomial curve), the extrema
are at the roots in (0, 1) of `N'D - ND'`. That polynomial is formed in the
Bernstein basis of degree `2n - 1` as a sum of coordinate differences times
positive weights (a power basis subtracts large products and loses the roots
when weights are 2^60 apart). Its roots come from recursion on the derivative,
which cuts [0, 1] into monotone intervals, and bisection. The box is that of
the curve evaluated at the ends and at those parameters. It is the curve's own
box to the rounding of the evaluation, not a conservative one.

## The hash spelling

`hash.h` states it once: FNV-1a 64 over fields written least significant byte
first by shifting, a `double` as its IEEE bit pattern. `shape-scene`,
`shape-cpu` and the sinks hash through `GSHGEOM_Hash`. The known answers are
in `tests/known_answers.h`, generated by `tools/gen-hash-vectors.py` (no code
shared with the library); the cross-target value over curves and paths is
`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`, produced by `tools/corpus-hash.c`.

## Maps

A map (`map.h`) is `M p + t` in 2D or 3D, with a kind tag. Maps are multiplied
in one function, and the order is fixed: `gshgeom_map_compose_chain`
takes the chain root first and the target last and forms
`target . root . ... . leaf` by composing from the root down and the target
last; every entry is `((a0 b0 + a1 b1) + a2 b2)` with no fused multiply-add, so
the bits do not depend on who composed. The tag is a set of the classes a map
was built from (translation, uniform or non-uniform scale, the canonical y
flip, another mirror, quarter turns, rotation, skew, singular, general); a
constructor sets it from its matrix, exactly (the sign of the determinant is
the exact predicate's), and a composition ORs the factors' tags, so a tag
never loses a class and may keep one the product has undone.

A rotation takes the source's angle unit. Degrees, gradians and turns are
reduced by an exact subtraction to within half a quarter turn, the quarter
part is applied by swapping and negating, and only the remainder reaches
`gmath_sin` and `gmath_cos`: a quarter turn is exactly 0 and +-1, and an angle
near a quarter keeps its leading digits. An angle in radians is not snapped.

The route predicate (`gshgeom_map_grid_aligned`) reads the tag and the values:
2D, tag within {translation, uniform scale, y flip}, and values exactly
`x' = s x + tx`, `y' = +-s y + ty`, `s > 0`. It returns the scale and
translation; whether a hinter takes that size is the caller's.

## Orientation and the exact predicates

`orient.h` states the rules once (a positive angle turns +x toward +y; positive
winding, positive signed area and positive `orient2d` are that sense; the left
of a tangent is the tangent turned by a negative angle). `orient2d` and
`orient3d` return the exact sign. A filter with Shewchuk's published static
error bounds decides the clear cases; the rest go to `src/exact`, which forms
the sum of products of the input doubles as an integer: each double is
`m 2^e`, each product of mantissas is a multi-word integer, the terms are
aligned to the smallest exponent and added in a 7,168-bit two's complement
accumulator. Nothing is rounded, so subnormals and values near `DBL_MAX` are
exact. The expansion is in the raw coordinates (no difference is formed first).

## The 26.6 device path and the flattener

`device.h` is the exit from model space. `gshgeom_round_26_6` is the one
conversion from `double`, `floor(x * 64 + 0.5)`, round half up: `x * 64` is
exact, so the floor and the fraction are exact, and no addition can round (the
double expression `floor(t + 0.5)` is wrong for the one `t` just below a half).
A result outside `int32` is `ERR_LIMIT`, never wrapped and never clipped;
clipping in `double` is a sink's decision. The device path is a builder over
`int32` points (move, line, quadratic, cubic, close; no weights, because 26.6 has
no rational form), and a verb after a close stores a move at the closed
subpath's start, so every stored subpath begins with a move.
`gshgeom_device_path_from_model` maps each point in `double` (`gshgeom_map_apply_point`)
and rounds it; every control point must round into range. A rational curve is
subdivided in `double` after the map, in homogeneous coordinates at the
parameter 1/2, until its projected control points are within 1/32 pixel of the
chord *segment* (convexity of positive weights makes that a bound), and becomes
lines.

**The integer spelling and the fixed-point builder (story 14a, AD-5).**
`gshgeom_scale_26_6(p, S, flip)` is the same rule for a point a fixed-point
source scales itself: a 26.6 design coordinate `p` times a 16.16 scale `S`
(pixels per unit times 2^16), negated when `flip` is 1 (y up to y down), is
`floor((s p S + 2^15) / 2^16)` 1/64 pixel, half up, in 64-bit integers, with no
shift of a negative value. Wherever it answers it equals `gshgeom_round_26_6` of
the `double` route `(s (p / 64)) (S / 65536)`: an answer fits an `int32`, so
`|p S| < 2^47 + 2^15`, and below `2^53` every step of that route is exact. A
result past the `int32` range is `ERR_LIMIT`, as in the `double` rule. It is what
a glyph path provider's fast tier rounds an unhinted point with, and why the
fast tier with hinting off is the general route under the snapped map bit for
bit (shape-scene's `glyph_provider.h`); the test is every residue of `p S` mod
2^16 in both signs and both flips, 400,000 random pairs over the whole `int32`
range, and the edges of the result. The fixed-point builder
(`gshgeom_path_move_to_fixed` and the three drawing verbs) takes `int32`
coordinates with 0 to 16 fraction bits and adds `v / 2^b`, which is exact in a
`double`, so a library that names no floating type (`font`, with 26.6 design
units) hands its coordinates to a model path without a rounding of its own.

`flatten.h` is the integer flattener, **revision 2**, ported from `font`'s
`src/raster/flatten.c` (as of `font` commit 69d1860; shape-geometry does not
depend on `font`, and `font` has since dropped its copy and calls this one).
Points are widened by eight
fractional bits and a curve is split at the midpoint of its widened control
points (each midpoint `floor((u + v + 1) / 2)`, half up) until `|2 p1 - p0 - p2|`,
or both `|3 p1 - 2 p0 - p3|` and `|3 p2 - p0 - 2 p3|`, summed over both axes,
is at most `4 * 256 * (t - 1)` widened units, i.e. `4 * (t - 1)` units for the
tolerance `t` (the default is 4 units, 1/16 pixel, `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_TOLERANCE`:
AD-12 makes it part of the definition, so the threshold is 12 units, 3,072
widened; a sink with a tolerance of its own passes it, at least
`GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_MIN_TOLERANCE`, 2); to at most 20 levels. A vertex is rounded to 26.6 once, half up (`floor((v + 128) / 256)`),
when it is emitted, and the end point of every piece is emitted, duplicates
included. The output is a flattened path: polylines, each with a closed flag,
the closing line implicit. All arithmetic is `int64`; no shift of a negative
value, no float, no libm.

### The guarantee and its proof

Every point of the curve is within `t - 0.12` units of the polyline (3.88 units
for `t = 4`), for every curve whose control points are any `int32` values, and
no piece is forced by the depth cap. The proof is stated above the code in
`src/flatten/flatten.c`; in outline: (a) all points stay between the inputs, so
widened coordinates are below 2^39 and every sum below 2^43; (b) a point made by
`k` halvings is within `1.5 k` widened of the exact de Casteljau point (30 at
depth 20); (c) a flat piece is within `T/4 = t - 1` units of its chord
(quadratic: `B - L = s(1-s)(2 Q1 - Q0 - Q2)`; cubic: `B - L = 3s(1-s)((1-s) e1 +
s e2)` with `|e|_2 <= |e|_1 = d/3`); (d) the piece built on the noisy points is
within `sqrt(2) * 30 / 256 = 0.166` units of the exact curve at the same
parameter; (e) narrowing moves a vertex, and a point of a chord, by at most
`sqrt(2)/2 = 0.7072`; (f) together `t - 0.1268 < t - 0.12`; (g) after 20
halvings a piece's exact deviation sum is at most 4 (quadratic) or 12 (cubic)
widened, the noise at most 160 or 360, against a threshold of 3,072: the cap
never binds. The proof holds for every tolerance from 2 units to `INT32_MAX`
(the threshold being derived from `t`, below 2^41 for any `int32`), and a
smaller one is refused with `ERR_INVALID` before anything is emitted, never
raised to 2. `font` takes a tolerance as an option and refuses 1 and below 0 with
`GFNT_ERR_INVALID` itself, so the flattener's refusal is a guard for it.

**The ways to ask.** `gshgeom_flatten_quadratic` and `gshgeom_flatten_cubic` flatten
one curve at a tolerance, handing each vertex to a callback together with the
parameter interval its piece covers (`leaf` and `level`: the piece is
`[leaf / 2^level, (leaf + 1) / 2^level]`) and stopping at the callback's first
refusal; a sink that places or flips its points before flattening (`font`: the half-up
midpoint is not symmetric under negation) uses these. `gshgeom_device_path_flatten`
flattens a device path at the default tolerance, and
`gshgeom_device_path_flatten_with_tolerance` at a stated one; the two are the same
code. `src/flatten/flatten_internal.h` has the same curve functions with a smaller depth
cap and the count of pieces it forced, which the tests need because the proof says
no input reaches the real cap.

`tests/unit/test_tolerance.cpp` checks it against the curve, not against pixels,
on the library's own pieces (the callback's `leaf` and `level` say which interval
each covers), at tolerances from 2 to 1000 and the largest: inside every piece the
flattener made, thirty-three exact points of the curve
(Bernstein form in `long double` at dyadic parameters) are measured to that
piece's segment, on every size class from coordinates below 2^4 up to the whole
`int32` range, and on the corners of the `int32` square (about 53,000 vertices
for a cubic); the smallest margin seen is recorded as a test property. The same
file pins vertex lists at tolerances 2, 3, 4 and 64 from an independent big-integer
Python model (this test began as `font`'s, when `font` had its own copy), checks
that the depth cap is counted when it is forced (with the internal smaller cap),
that the pieces tile the curve, that a callback's refusal stops the flattening,
and that the path functions agree with the curve functions at every tolerance.
`tests/unit/test_flatten.cpp` does the default tolerance through the path, where
the piece intervals are recovered by a model of the recursion in the test, which
must first reproduce the library's vertices exactly; nine vertex lists there are
pinned from the same kind of model (`tools/gen-hash-vectors.py`, `flatten_model`),
which catches a wrong rounding rule that stays inside the bound (a floor midpoint
at eight extra bits does).

### The revision

The flattener is versioned: `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_REVISION` and
`gshgeom_flatten_revision()` (2). It is a number recorded where a rebaseline
lives (the header of a golden, `tests/flatten_vectors.h`), checked against the
code, and in no hash: the flattened path's spelling and its format version (1) are
unchanged, and two revisions that gave equal output hash equal. There is no
selector for an older revision.

| Revision | What it was |
|---|---|
| 1 | `font`'s flattener as ported in story 7, bit for bit: every midpoint floored to 26.6, flat at `4 * t` units, depth 16. The midpoints' error accumulated with the depth, so against the exact curve it reached 2 to 4 units on a tiny curve, 7.7 below 2^16 and 11 to 13 on a large one (13.1 over the `int32` range), where 4 was promised. |
| 2 | Widened by eight bits, one rounding of each vertex, flat at `4 * (t - 1)`, depth 20, proved within `t - 0.12`. Story 7a. |

`tests/flatten_vectors.h` holds 960 curves with the vertices `font`'s flattener
(revision 2, tolerance 4) produced for them; `tools/vectors-from-font/` records
how (it is the only place `font` is named; nothing here includes or links it),
and `tools/gen-hash-vectors.py --check-flatten tests/flatten_vectors.h` runs the
independent model over all of them. The cross-target hash of the corpus probe
(`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`) was re-measured for this revision.

## Along-path parametrization

`along.h`. A *measured path* is built once from a model path and queried by
distance. The distance along a path is the sum of the arc lengths of its
segments in element order; a move adds nothing (the point jumps, the distance
does not), a close is the straight segment back to the start, and a segment of
length exactly zero has no extent and is never selected. A distance that is
exactly the boundary between two segments (or two subpaths) belongs to the later
one, so a query is a right-hand limit, except the length itself, which is the end
of the last segment. A distance outside `[0, length]` is refused by `_at` and
clamped by `_at_clamped` (NaN and infinity are refused by both).

The length of a line is a hypotenuse. The length of a curve is the integral of
its speed `|B'(t)|` (for a rational curve `B' = (N'D - ND') / D^2`, computed from
the homogeneous numerator and denominator by de Casteljau's recurrences) by
adaptive eight-point Gauss-Legendre: a panel is accepted when it differs from the
sum of its two halves by at most `tol * S * width`, `S` the length of the control
polygon, and the two halves are then the rows of the table. A row holds the
segment, the parameter interval, the cumulative distance at its start and its
length, so a query is a binary search and a safeguarded Newton solve inside one
panel. The tolerance is `0x1p-40` by default and may be set from `0x1p-46` to
`0x1p-4`; below the minimum the estimate would measure the rounding of the sum.
The estimate is the difference between two evaluations of a smooth function, so
it is evidence rather than proof; the tests measure the real error against an
independent MPFR reference (`tools/gen-along-vectors.py`, in the pinned oracle
image), and against closed forms: a circular arc is a rational quadratic, whose
length is `r * theta`.

At a point where the velocity vanishes (a cusp, or a control point repeated at an
end) the tangent is the direction of the first nonzero derivative of
`E = N'D - ND'`, which has the direction of `B'`, on the later side: the
right-hand limit, and at `t = 1` the left-hand limit. The normal is
`gshgeom_normal_left(tangent)` in 2D (the rule of `orient.h`, so on a positively
wound circle it points out) and absent in 3D, where left has no meaning. The rows
are bounded by `max_points` of the limits.

Everything is `+ - * /`, `gmath_hypot` and a fixed order of operations, so the
table, every query, the hash and the dump are the same bits on every target; the
cross-target probe measures 90 random paths of every kind at three tolerances and
hashes every number of every answer.

## The fill rules

`fill.h` holds the two fill rules of the family and nothing else, so that a
library with no use for the rest of this one (`font`, whose headers carry no
floating-point type) can include it alone: `GSHGEOM_FILL_NONZERO` (0, the
default, and what every font format means) and `GSHGEOM_FILL_EVEN_ODD` (1),
`GSHGEOM_FILL_RULE_COUNT` closing the enumeration, and `gshgeom_fill_rule_string`.
The values are pinned; a further rule is appended after them. The
rule is applied by `shape-cpu`; `font`'s `GFNT_FillRule` is this type under its
old name.

## Strokes

`stroke.h` states what a stroke covers, once, as rules SG1 to SG18, and
`gshgeom_stroke_expand` turns a centerline, the parameters and a map into an
outline whose nonzero fill is that region within the tolerance. The rules are
the normative text; this section records where they come from, the readings of
the texts they follow, the construction and its proof in outline, and the
revision.

### Sources

The rules follow SVG 2 and the WHATWG HTML standard's canvas line styles, each
pinned (`tools/svg2-texts/fetch.sh` fetches them and checks their SHA-256): the
SVG 2 Candidate Recommendation of 2018-10-04 (its painting and paths chapters),
the SVG 2 editor's draft at `w3c/svgwg` commit `f846f12e3f50`, the SVG Strokes
module draft at the same commit, and the WHATWG HTML source at commit
`c6c3055c1287`. The editor's draft has withdrawn the `miter-clip` and `arcs`
values of `stroke-linejoin`; the 2018 Candidate Recommendation is the one text
that defines them, and SG8 follows it.

Where the texts leave a point open, the rules settle it by the limit of the
cases they define, and say so:

| Point | Reading |
|---|---|

### The construction (revision 1)

The region is a union of pieces: the body of a stretch of a segment, a join, a
cap, a degenerate shape. Each piece is emitted as the convex hull of a few core
points it computes, inflated by an octagon and rounded to 26.6, as positively
wound polygons (src/stroke/emit.c). A piece states two bounds: how far the
region can be outside its core hull (`d_in`) and how far the core hull can be
outside the region (`d_out`). The inflation is `d_in` plus the rounding's reach,
so after the rounding the polygon still contains its piece; the outward reach is
`d_out + 1.0826 (d_in + rho) + rho`, `rho` the rounding and arithmetic allowance,
and a piece is emitted only when that is within `tau - 1/4` units. The outline
therefore contains the region and lies within `tau - 1/4` units of it, and no
polygon winds negatively, so overlaps never cancel (SG13).

Lines, joins, caps and degenerate shapes are polygons of their own corners, or
circular sectors drawn as the control polygons of halved rational quadratic arcs
(no angle is formed). A curve segment is split by de Casteljau at the parameter
7/16 until each piece is a **band** (both cones of its hodograph narrower than a
quarter turn, its curvature bound times `h` below the cosine of the cone's width,
so no two normals meet and each rail is a graph over its chord within
`(lambda / 2) sin(alpha)`) or a **fan** (a short piece: the double sector of its
normal cone about its start, or a disc when its end normals are nearly opposite,
which is how a cusp gets its disc). Errors of the doubles are carried with every
coefficient. A piece still refused after 64 splits is `ERR_LIMIT`, never drawn
coarser. The arcs join is built from its boundary: the straight edges and the
circumscribed polylines of the extensions' quarter-turn sub-arcs (each within
the allowed distance of its arc), cut by the clip's half plane (the boundary's
parts inside it, with the gaps closed along the clip line, where chords that run
both ways over the same stretch cancel), then cut into slabs at every height an
edge ends or two edges cross, and each stretch of nonzero winding between
neighbouring edges is one trapezoid. Every piece is positively wound; the
boundary may have at most 2048 edges, past which the join is `ERR_LIMIT`. The
arcs join's clip is the one place `math`'s trigonometry is used.

The proof is above the code: emit.c (the theorem and the rounding), body.c (bands
and fans), join.c (SG9's exact decision and the miter tip's error), arc.c (arcs
and the arcs join). Its arithmetic bound assumes every magnitude is within
`2^30` pixels (anchors, the mapped control points' offsets, the half width and
the miter tip); a larger one is `ERR_LIMIT`.

### What checks it

`tests/stroke_oracle.h` decides membership from the rules alone, in
`long double`, sharing no code with the expansion, and samples a superset of the
region's boundary at 1/256 pixel. `tests/unit/test_stroke_oracle.cpp` checks it
first where the region is the set of points within `h` of the centerline (round
joins and caps), sees it fail with a sector on the inner side, and checks it
against the exact verdicts of `tools/gen-stroke-vectors.py` (an independent model
in mpmath, run in the pinned oracle image). `tests/unit/test_stroke_matrix.cpp`
compares the outline with the oracle at every decided point over the case matrix,
checks that no polygon winds negatively and every vertex is inside the
conservative stroke box plus the tolerance, and records the deviation profile.
`tests/unit/test_stroke.cpp` covers the parameters, statuses, parts, hash, dump,
limits and allocation failure.

### Dashes

A dash array cuts the centerline before the body is built (`stroke.h`, SG19 to
SG30). Each dash is an open piece of the centerline and is stroked by the rules
of the solid stroke (SG5 to SG10), so a dashed stroke is the same union of
positively wound pieces and keeps the same tolerance proof; only the ends of
each piece and the vertices inside it are new. `src/stroke/dash.c` does it.

**The distance and the cut.** Lengths are measured with `along.h` at its finest
tolerance (`2^-46`), in the width space: the model path under local width, the
mapped path under device width (the expansion rebuilds the centerline element for
element under the map and measures that). A dash end at a distance becomes a
segment and a parameter through the measured table (a boundary is the later
segment's, the end of a subpath the last segment's); the piece of a segment
between two parameters is cut by de Casteljau in homogeneous coordinates, the
outer end points copied bit for bit, so a piece that keeps an end of its segment
keeps that vertex exactly. The join at a vertex strictly inside a dash is decided
from the two original segments that meet there (SG22), never from the cut
pieces, whose computed control points would show a smooth vertex as a tiny turn;
a cap's direction at a cut is the hodograph of the original segment at the
parameter (`N'D - ND'` for a rational kind), or SG5's exact end direction when the
cut is an end of the segment. A dot (SG24) takes the same direction.

**The positions** (SG21) are SVG 2's "dash positions", with a running position
replaced by `(q P + S) - o` so that the error of an end does not grow with the
dashes before it, and the phase reduced by exact long division (no quotient in
doubles). One tie differs from the CR's loop and is the only difference: the CR
takes the least index whose prefix sum is at least the phase (`>=`), so a phase
exactly at the end of a positive dash leaves a dash of no length at the start of
the path, which round and square caps draw as a dot. Every shipping renderer draws
nothing there, and the literal rule leaves a valid DXF linetype that begins with a
space unlowerable (the line would start exactly at the end of the dash before it).
A positive dash that the phase has used up entirely is therefore over; a dash of no
length exactly at the phase is kept, and a dash that would begin exactly at the end
of a subpath is not made (the CR's `while position < pathlength`), so the
convention is half open at both ends.

| Point | Reading |
|---|---|
| A closed subpath | The CR defines each dash as a pair of distances within the subpath, with its own caps, and no join at the start of a closed subpath. A dash that crosses the start point is read as one piece (the limit of the defined cases: a dash that nothing cuts stays whole): when the first piece began before the path and the last dash would go on past its end they are one dash with a join at the start vertex and caps only at its two ends. A dash that ends exactly at the start, or begins exactly there, stays two, each with its cap; a dash that would cover the whole subpath is the closed stroke. |
| A zero-length dash | Two caps back to back (the text's start cap and end cap at one position; Canvas's "two caps placed back-to-back"): a disc, a square along the tangent, nothing for butt. It is drawn whatever `degenerate` says (Canvas omits zero-length subpaths and draws dotted lines). |
| The tangent of a dot | The right-hand limit, as along.h's boundary rule: the segment that begins at the point; at the end of the subpath the last segment's end direction. |
| One pattern for the path | The pattern runs over the path distance and is cut at the end of each subpath (a dash does not cross the jump of a move); the phase at a subpath is the offset plus the distance before it. |
| A degenerate subpath | The first piece of SG21 for a length of zero: a dot at its point when the pattern is on there, with SG11's effective tangent; omitted when `degenerate` says omit. |
| Alignment | The clip region is of the undashed closed subpaths; the aligned region is the dashed centered region at twice the width. |

**The cut position's error** (SG26). The error of a distance is `along.h`'s
contract (its estimate of a segment's error is at most the tolerance times the
control polygon's length; the running sum adds `rows 2^-53` of itself; the solve
inside a panel stops at `2^-52` of its length) plus the pattern's arithmetic; the
call refuses (`ERR_LIMIT`) unless that, times the map's norm, is at most `2^-20`
pixel. A dash end that moves by `delta` along the centerline moves every boundary
point of its end by at most `(1 + h kappa) delta`, so the cut costs at most
`(1 + h kappa) 2^-20` pixel of the outline's error, inside the reserve the
construction keeps of the tolerance wherever `h kappa <= 2^12`. The measurer's bound
is an estimate and not a proof; the tests compare the cuts with an independent
quadrature (1024 panels of 8-point Gauss-Legendre, long double) and the circle's
closed form.

**What checks it.** `tests/stroke_oracle.h` defines a dashed stroke from the text
alone (its own quadrature, the positions transcribed with a running position, cut
segments, dots, the dash across the start) and the matrix of
`tests/unit/test_stroke_matrix.cpp` compares the outline with it on lines, every
curve kind, closed and open subpaths, every join and cap, both scopes, both width
spaces and alignment. `tools/gen-dash-vectors.py` states the positions and the DXF
lowering in exact rationals; `test_dash.cpp` compares them bit for bit and checks
the dash across the start at exact points.

**DXF simple linetypes** (SG30, `gshgeom_stroke_dashes_from_dxf`). A pattern is a
list of elements (DXF group 49): a positive value is a dash with the pen down for
that length, a negative value a space, zero a dot. The sign convention is the
documented one for linetype definitions (for example the ezdxf linetype tutorial:
`> 0` a line, `< 0` a gap, `0` a dot; Autodesk's reference calls group 49 a "dash,
dot or space length"). The pattern repeats from the start of the line, so the last
run and the first are one run when they are of one kind, and the line starts inside
it: that is the offset. A pattern that begins with a space is lowered with the line
starting at the end of the dash before it (the half-open tie above). One that begins
with a space and ends with a dot has the dot of each period exactly where the line
starts, and the positions keep a dash of no length at the phase (the ordinary dotted
line, `[0, 10]` at offset 0, needs it), so its offset is the limit of the phases after
the dot instead: the next double above the dot's position in the array (for a lone dot,
the period times `2^-52`). That moves the start of the line by one unit in the last place
of that position, far inside the cut error the expansion already allows (SG26), and puts
the dot behind it; the pattern is otherwise exact, and no style field is needed. A
rule keyed to the array's first element, or to how the offset was reached, would draw
one periodic pattern differently depending on where the array was cut. Complex linetypes (embedded
shapes or text) are not lowered. The vectors are hand-built patterns that cover
every case of the merge and the rotation (dotted, dashed, dash-dot, divide, center,
phantom and border shapes, a space first, a dash last, a dot first, spaces and dots
only, a scale); the lowering is also checked by its definition (the pen-down
intervals of the raw pattern equal those of the lowered array under the positions).
