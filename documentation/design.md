**Status:** parts 1 and 2 of the shape-geometry plan: curves, model paths,
bounds and the hash spelling; maps, the orientation rules, the exact
predicates and the route predicate. The 26.6 device path and the flattener are

Paths, transforms and flattening for the shape family, bit-identical on every host.

The dependency rules and the determinism gates that bind this library are in

## Model

Model space is `double`, y down. A curve (`curve.h`) is a value: a kind (line,
quadratic, cubic, rational quadratic, rational cubic), a dimension (2 or 3),
control points and, for the rational kinds, a positive weight at each. A path
(`path.h`) is the opaque builder: `move_to`, the drawing verbs, `close`.

Every number is checked when it enters: NaN and infinity are `ERR_INVALID`, a
finite number beyond 2^100 (or a weight outside 2^-100 .. 2^100) is
`ERR_LIMIT`. That bound is what lets every evaluation and every bound be
finite without a check at the end. A failed call leaves a path unchanged.

## Bounds

The box of a curve is the box of the curve, not of its control polygon. For
each coordinate, written `N/D` (`D = 1` for a polynomial curve), the extrema
are at the roots in (0, 1) of `N'D - ND'`. That polynomial is formed in the
Bernstein basis of degree `2n - 1` as a sum of coordinate differences times
positive weights (a power basis subtracts large products and loses the roots
when weights are 2^60 apart). Its roots come from recursion on the derivative,
which cuts [0, 1] into monotone intervals, and bisection. The box is that of
the curve evaluated at the ends and at those parameters. It is the curve's own
box to the rounding of the evaluation, not a conservative one.

## The hash spelling

`hash.h` states it once: FNV-1a 64 over fields written least significant byte
first by shifting, a `double` as its IEEE bit pattern. `shape-scene`,
`shape-cpu` and the sinks hash through `GSHGEOM_Hash`. The known answers are
in `tests/known_answers.h`, generated by `tools/gen-hash-vectors.py` (no code
shared with the library); the cross-target value over curves and paths is
`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`, produced by `tools/corpus-hash.c`.

## Maps

A map (`map.h`) is `M p + t` in 2D or 3D, with a kind tag. Maps are multiplied
in one function, and the order is the spine's: `gshgeom_map_compose_chain`
takes the chain root first and the target last and forms
`target . root . ... . leaf` by composing from the root down and the target
last; every entry is `((a0 b0 + a1 b1) + a2 b2)` with no fused multiply-add, so
the bits do not depend on who composed. The tag is a set of the classes a map
was built from (translation, uniform or non-uniform scale, the canonical y
flip, another mirror, quarter turns, rotation, skew, singular, general); a
constructor sets it from its matrix, exactly (the sign of the determinant is
the exact predicate's), and a composition ORs the factors' tags, so a tag
never loses a class and may keep one the product has undone.

A rotation takes the source's angle unit. Degrees, gradians and turns are
reduced by an exact subtraction to within half a quarter turn, the quarter
part is applied by swapping and negating, and only the remainder reaches
`gmath_sin` and `gmath_cos`: a quarter turn is exactly 0 and +-1, and an angle
near a quarter keeps its leading digits. An angle in radians is not snapped.

The route predicate (`gshgeom_map_grid_aligned`) reads the tag and the values:
2D, tag within {translation, uniform scale, y flip}, and values exactly
`x' = s x + tx`, `y' = +-s y + ty`, `s > 0`. It returns the scale and
translation; whether a hinter takes that size is the caller's.

## Orientation and the exact predicates

`orient.h` states the rules once (a positive angle turns +x toward +y; positive
winding, positive signed area and positive `orient2d` are that sense; the left
of a tangent is the tangent turned by a negative angle). `orient2d` and
`orient3d` return the exact sign. A filter with Shewchuk's published static
error bounds decides the clear cases; the rest go to `src/exact`, which forms
the sum of products of the input doubles as an integer: each double is
`m 2^e`, each product of mantissas is a multi-word integer, the terms are
aligned to the smallest exponent and added in a 7,168-bit two's complement
accumulator. Nothing is rounded, so subnormals and values near `DBL_MAX` are
exact. The expansion is in the raw coordinates (no difference is formed first).

## Tests and gates

`make test` runs the unit tests and the gates; `make check-planted` builds
each defect in `tools/check-planted.py` and requires a test to fail on it
(the defects it accepts as invisible, with reasons, are in its docstring).
`suite/tools/xarch/shape.sh` (section 6) and `suite/tools/xwin/shape.sh` run
the probe on every target.
