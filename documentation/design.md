# Design

**Status:** parts 1 to 4 of the shape-geometry plan: curves, model paths,
bounds and the hash spelling; maps, the orientation rules, the exact
predicates and the route predicate; the 26.6 device path and the integer
flattener (with the conic route of rational curves and arcs);
along-path parametrization; path vertices; exact elliptical arcs (the element,
three constructors, the exact decomposition and everything that reads a path);
the fill rules; strokes: the definition of the covered region and the expansion
solid and dashed (SG19 to SG30), and a DXF simple
linetype lowered to a dash array.

Paths, transforms and flattening for the shape family, bit-identical on every host.

The dependency rules and the determinism gates that bind this library are in
the Makefile's header comment and in `tools/`: `check-fp-contract`,
`check-edges`, `check-libm` and `check-no-x87`, each shown to fail on a planted
violation by `make check-gates`.

## Model

Model space is `double`, y down. A curve (`curve.h`) is a value: a kind (line,
quadratic, cubic, rational quadratic, rational cubic), a dimension (2 or 3),
control points and, for the rational kinds, a positive weight at each. A path
(`path.h`) is the opaque builder: `move_to`, the drawing verbs, `close`.

Every number is checked when it enters: NaN and infinity are `ERR_INVALID`, a
finite number beyond 2^100 (or a weight outside 2^-100 .. 2^100) is
`ERR_LIMIT`. That bound is what lets every evaluation and every bound be
finite without a check at the end. A failed call leaves a path unchanged.

## Bounds

The box of a curve is the box of the curve, not of its control polygon. For
each coordinate, written `N/D` (`D = 1` for a polynomial curve), the extrema
are at the roots in (0, 1) of `N'D - ND'`. That polynomial is formed in the
Bernstein basis of degree `2n - 1` as a sum of coordinate differences times
positive weights (a power basis subtracts large products and loses the roots
when weights are 2^60 apart). Its roots come from recursion on the derivative,
which cuts [0, 1] into monotone intervals, and bisection. The box is that of
the curve evaluated at the ends and at those parameters. It is the curve's own
box to the rounding of the evaluation, not a conservative one.

## The hash spelling

`hash.h` states it once: FNV-1a 64 over fields written least significant byte
first by shifting, a `double` as its IEEE bit pattern. `shape-scene`,
`shape-cpu` and the sinks hash through `GSHGEOM_Hash`. The known answers are
in `tests/known_answers.h`, generated by `tools/gen-hash-vectors.py` (no code
shared with the library); the cross-target value over curves and paths is
`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`, produced by `tools/corpus-hash.c`.

## Maps

A map (`map.h`) is `M p + t` in 2D or 3D, with a kind tag. Maps are multiplied
in one function, and the order is fixed: `gshgeom_map_compose_chain`
takes the chain root first and the target last and forms
`target . root . ... . leaf` by composing from the root down and the target
last; every entry is `((a0 b0 + a1 b1) + a2 b2)` with no fused multiply-add, so
the bits do not depend on who composed. The tag is a set of the classes a map
was built from (translation, uniform or non-uniform scale, the canonical y
flip, another mirror, quarter turns, rotation, skew, singular, general); a
constructor sets it from its matrix, exactly (the sign of the determinant is
the exact predicate's), and a composition ORs the factors' tags, so a tag
never loses a class and may keep one the product has undone.

A rotation takes the source's angle unit. Degrees, gradians and turns are
reduced by an exact subtraction to within half a quarter turn, the quarter
part is applied by swapping and negating, and only the remainder reaches
`gmath_sin` and `gmath_cos`: a quarter turn is exactly 0 and +-1, and an angle
near a quarter keeps its leading digits. An angle in radians is not snapped.

The route predicate (`gshgeom_map_grid_aligned`) reads the tag and the values:
2D, tag within {translation, uniform scale, y flip}, and values exactly
`x' = s x + tx`, `y' = +-s y + ty`, `s > 0`. It returns the scale and
translation; whether a hinter takes that size is the caller's.

**Whether a map stretches every direction** (`gshgeom_map_stretches_every_direction`) is the predicate a sampler chooses
between its magnifying and its minifying filter by. It is true exactly when the smallest singular value of the 2x2 linear
part is greater than 1. With the columns `a = (m00, m10)` and `b = (m01, m11)` and `A = a.a`, `B = a.b`, `C = b.b`, that is
`A > 1` and `(A - 1)(C - 1) > B^2` (then `C > 1` follows: the two factors have one sign), decided on the entries' own
doubles in the exact sum of products that the orientation predicates use, so there is no rounding in the products and no
square root. `(A - 1)(C - 1) - B^2` is `D^2 - A - C + 1` with `D = m00 m11 - m01 m10`, by Lagrange's identity. An exactly
unit scale does not stretch, and neither does a map that stretches one direction and shrinks another: a determinant, an area
ratio, would call a 7 by 0.3 map a stretch. A 3D map is judged by the plane's linear part, and the translation does not
matter. `tools/gen-stretch-vectors.py` decides 439 cases in exact rationals and writes `tests/stretch_vectors.h`; the
cross-target probe folds the predicate over 600 maps into a hash and count of their own (`predicate-hash`,
`predicate-count`), so the first pair never moves.

## Orientation and the exact predicates

`orient.h` states the rules once (a positive angle turns +x toward +y; positive
winding, positive signed area and positive `orient2d` are that sense; the left
of a tangent is the tangent turned by a negative angle). `orient2d` and
`orient3d` return the exact sign. A filter with Shewchuk's published static
error bounds decides the clear cases; the rest go to `src/exact`, which forms
the sum of products of the input doubles as an integer: each double is
`m 2^e`, each product of mantissas is a multi-word integer, the terms are
aligned to the smallest exponent and added in a 7,168-bit two's complement
accumulator. Nothing is rounded, so subnormals and values near `DBL_MAX` are
exact. The expansion is in the raw coordinates (no difference is formed first).

## The 26.6 device path and the flattener

`device.h` is the exit from model space. `gshgeom_round_26_6` is the one
conversion from `double`, `floor(x * 64 + 0.5)`, round half up: `x * 64` is
exact, so the floor and the fraction are exact, and no addition can round (the
double expression `floor(t + 0.5)` is wrong for the one `t` just below a half).
A result outside `int32` is `ERR_LIMIT`, never wrapped and never clipped;
clipping in `double` is a sink's decision. The device path is a builder over
`int32` points (move, line, quadratic, cubic, close; no weights, because 26.6 has
no rational form), and a verb after a close stores a move at the closed
subpath's start, so every stored subpath begins with a move.
`gshgeom_device_path_from_model` maps each point in `double` (`gshgeom_map_apply_point`)
and rounds it; every control point must round into range. A rational curve (a
rational quadratic, a rational cubic, and each piece of an arc) takes the conic
route, below: it is subdivided in `double` after the map, in homogeneous
coordinates at the parameter 1/2, until its projected control points are within
`t - 1` units of the chord *segment* (convexity of positive weights makes that a
bound), and becomes lines.

**The integer spelling and the fixed-point builder (story 14a, AD-5).**
`gshgeom_scale_26_6(p, S, flip)` is the same rule for a point a fixed-point
source scales itself: a 26.6 design coordinate `p` times a 16.16 scale `S`
(pixels per unit times 2^16), negated when `flip` is 1 (y up to y down), is
`floor((s p S + 2^15) / 2^16)` 1/64 pixel, half up, in 64-bit integers, with no
shift of a negative value. Wherever it answers it equals `gshgeom_round_26_6` of
the `double` route `(s (p / 64)) (S / 65536)`: an answer fits an `int32`, so
`|p S| < 2^47 + 2^15`, and below `2^53` every step of that route is exact. A
result past the `int32` range is `ERR_LIMIT`, as in the `double` rule. It is what
a glyph path provider's fast tier rounds an unhinted point with, and why the
fast tier with hinting off is the general route under the snapped map bit for
bit (shape-scene's `glyph_provider.h`); the test is every residue of `p S` mod
2^16 in both signs and both flips, 400,000 random pairs over the whole `int32`
range, and the edges of the result. The fixed-point builder
(`gshgeom_path_move_to_fixed` and the three drawing verbs) takes `int32`
coordinates with 0 to 16 fraction bits and adds `v / 2^b`, which is exact in a
`double`, so a library that names no floating type (`font`, with 26.6 design
units) hands its coordinates to a model path without a rounding of its own.

`flatten.h` is the integer flattener, ported from `font`'s
`src/raster/flatten.c` (as of `font` commit 69d1860; shape-geometry does not
depend on `font`, and `font` has since dropped its copy and calls this one).
Points are widened by eight
fractional bits and a curve is split at the midpoint of its widened control
points (each midpoint `floor((u + v + 1) / 2)`, half up) until `|2 p1 - p0 - p2|`,
or both `|3 p1 - 2 p0 - p3|` and `|3 p2 - p0 - 2 p3|`, summed over both axes,
is at most `4 * 256 * (t - 1)` widened units, i.e. `4 * (t - 1)` units for the
tolerance `t` (the default is 4 units, 1/16 pixel, `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_TOLERANCE`:
AD-12 makes it part of the definition, so the threshold is 12 units, 3,072
widened; a sink with a tolerance of its own passes it, at least
`GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_MIN_TOLERANCE`, 2); to at most 20 levels. A vertex is rounded to 26.6 once, half up (`floor((v + 128) / 256)`),
when it is emitted, and the end point of every piece is emitted, duplicates
included. The output is a flattened path: polylines, each with a closed flag,
the closing line implicit. All arithmetic is `int64`; no shift of a negative
value, no float, no libm.

### The guarantee and its proof

Every point of the curve is within `t - 0.12` units of the polyline (3.88 units
for `t = 4`), for every curve whose control points are any `int32` values, and
no piece is forced by the depth cap. The proof is stated above the code in
`src/flatten/flatten.c`; in outline: (a) all points stay between the inputs, so
widened coordinates are below 2^39 and every sum below 2^43; (b) a point made by
`k` halvings is within `1.5 k` widened of the exact de Casteljau point (30 at
depth 20); (c) a flat piece is within `T/4 = t - 1` units of its chord
(quadratic: `B - L = s(1-s)(2 Q1 - Q0 - Q2)`; cubic: `B - L = 3s(1-s)((1-s) e1 +
s e2)` with `|e|_2 <= |e|_1 = d/3`); (d) the piece built on the noisy points is
within `sqrt(2) * 30 / 256 = 0.166` units of the exact curve at the same
parameter; (e) narrowing moves a vertex, and a point of a chord, by at most
`sqrt(2)/2 = 0.7072`; (f) together `t - 0.1268 < t - 0.12`; (g) after 20
halvings a piece's exact deviation sum is at most 4 (quadratic) or 12 (cubic)
widened, the noise at most 160 or 360, against a threshold of 3,072: the cap
never binds. The proof holds for every tolerance from 2 units to `INT32_MAX`
(the threshold being derived from `t`, below 2^41 for any `int32`), and a
smaller one is refused with `ERR_INVALID` before anything is emitted, never
raised to 2. `font` takes a tolerance as an option and refuses 1 and below 0 with
`GFNT_ERR_INVALID` itself, so the flattener's refusal is a guard for it.

**The ways to ask.** `gshgeom_flatten_quadratic` and `gshgeom_flatten_cubic` flatten
one curve at a tolerance, handing each vertex to a callback together with the
parameter interval its piece covers (`leaf` and `level`: the piece is
`[leaf / 2^level, (leaf + 1) / 2^level]`) and stopping at the callback's first
refusal; a sink that places or flips its points before flattening (`font`: the half-up
midpoint is not symmetric under negation) uses these. `gshgeom_device_path_flatten`
flattens a device path at the default tolerance, and
`gshgeom_device_path_flatten_with_tolerance` at a stated one; the two are the same
code. `src/flatten/flatten_internal.h` has the same curve functions with a smaller depth
cap and the count of pieces it forced, which the tests need because the proof says
no input reaches the real cap.

`tests/unit/test_tolerance.cpp` checks it against the curve, not against pixels,
on the library's own pieces (the callback's `leaf` and `level` say which interval
each covers), at tolerances from 2 to 1000 and the largest: inside every piece the
flattener made, thirty-three exact points of the curve
(Bernstein form in `long double` at dyadic parameters) are measured to that
piece's segment, on every size class from coordinates below 2^4 up to the whole
`int32` range, and on the corners of the `int32` square (about 53,000 vertices
for a cubic); the smallest margin seen is recorded as a test property. The same
file pins vertex lists at tolerances 2, 3, 4 and 64 from an independent big-integer
Python model (this test began as `font`'s, when `font` had its own copy), checks
that the depth cap is counted when it is forced (with the internal smaller cap),
that the pieces tile the curve, that a callback's refusal stops the flattening,
and that the path functions agree with the curve functions at every tolerance.
`tests/unit/test_flatten.cpp` does the default tolerance through the path, where
the piece intervals are recovered by a model of the recursion in the test, which
must first reproduce the library's vertices exactly; nine vertex lists there are
pinned from the same kind of model (`tools/gen-hash-vectors.py`, `flatten_model`),
which catches a wrong rounding rule that stays inside the bound (a floor midpoint
at eight extra bits does).

**The canonical map** (SG31, `gshgeom_stroke_canonical_map`). The expansion rounds every vertex to 26.6 after the
map, so its bits depend on the map's translation, and an expansion shared between translations would be the
expansion of another one. The region does not depend on position at all, so a sink splits the translation: the
whole pixels `(floor tx, floor ty)` and a fraction in `[0, 1)`, expands at the map with the fraction only, and draws
the outline moved by the whole pixels. The outline is then a function of the centerline, the parameters, the linear
part and the fraction, the same bits at every whole-pixel translation (a translation that is not exactly a whole
number of pixels from another has another fraction: `0.3 + 64` rounds in `double`), which is what lets a cache of
expansions serve a translated stroke and a hit be the miss bit for bit. The split is exact: `t - floor t` is exact
below 2^52; for a tiny negative `t` the difference would round to 1, which is taken as 0 with the whole pixel one
more (the error is far below a 26.6 unit); a translation past 2^40 is `ERR_LIMIT`. It is one function here, so that
every sink that wants these bits (the CPU sink, the test sink) calls the same code.

### The revision

The flattener is versioned: `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_REVISION` and
`gshgeom_flatten_revision()` (1) number its definition. It is a number recorded
where a rebaseline lives (the header of a golden, `tests/flatten_vectors.h`),
checked against the code, and in no hash: the flattened path's spelling and its
format version (1) do not contain it. There is no selector for another revision.

The definition is the one above. The integer flattener widens by eight bits,
rounds each vertex once, is flat at `4 * (t - 1)`, has depth 20 and is proved
within `t - 0.12`. The device path of a rational curve or an arc goes by the
conic route, flat at `t - 1` units, the cap 240, proved within `t - 0.29`.

`tests/flatten_vectors.h` holds 960 curves with the vertices `font`'s flattener
(tolerance 4) produced for them; `tools/vectors-from-font/` records
how (it is the only place `font` is named; nothing here includes or links it),
and `tools/gen-hash-vectors.py --check-flatten tests/flatten_vectors.h` runs the
independent model over all of them. The cross-target hash of the corpus probe
(`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`) is pinned.

## Along-path parametrization

`along.h`. A *measured path* is built once from a model path and queried by
distance. The distance along a path is the sum of the arc lengths of its
segments in element order; a move adds nothing (the point jumps, the distance
does not), a close is the straight segment back to the start, and a segment of
length exactly zero has no extent and is never selected. A distance that is
exactly the boundary between two segments (or two subpaths) belongs to the later
one, so a query is a right-hand limit, except the length itself, which is the end
of the last segment. A distance outside `[0, length]` is refused by `_at` and
clamped by `_at_clamped` (NaN and infinity are refused by both).

The length of a line is a hypotenuse. The length of a curve is the integral of
its speed `|B'(t)|` (for a rational curve `B' = (N'D - ND') / D^2`, computed from
the homogeneous numerator and denominator by de Casteljau's recurrences) by
adaptive eight-point Gauss-Legendre: a panel is accepted when it differs from the
sum of its two halves by at most `tol * S * width`, `S` the length of the control
polygon, and the two halves are then the rows of the table. A row holds the
segment, the parameter interval, the cumulative distance at its start and its
length, so a query is a binary search and a safeguarded Newton solve inside one
panel. The tolerance is `0x1p-40` by default and may be set from `0x1p-46` to
`0x1p-4`; below the minimum the estimate would measure the rounding of the sum.
The estimate is the difference between two evaluations of a smooth function, so
it is evidence rather than proof; the tests measure the real error against an
independent MPFR reference (`tools/gen-along-vectors.py`, in the pinned oracle
image), and against closed forms: a circular arc is a rational quadratic, whose
length is `r * theta`.

At a point where the velocity vanishes (a cusp, or a control point repeated at an
end) the tangent is the direction of the first nonzero derivative of
`E = N'D - ND'`, which has the direction of `B'`, on the later side: the
right-hand limit, and at `t = 1` the left-hand limit. The normal is
`gshgeom_normal_left(tangent)` in 2D (the rule of `orient.h`, so on a positively
wound circle it points out) and absent in 3D, where left has no meaning. The rows
are bounded by `max_points` of the limits.

Everything is `+ - * /`, `gmath_hypot` and a fixed order of operations, so the
table, every query, the hash and the dump are the same bits on every target; the
cross-target probe measures 90 random paths of every kind at three tolerances and
hashes every number of every answer.

## Path vertices and the directions at them

`vertices.h` gives a 2D path's vertices in element order, each with its position, the unit incoming and outgoing
directions and the direction an `orient="auto"` marker takes. A vertex is the end point of an element (a move's point,
a segment's last control point, a close's subpath start); a drawing verb after a close starts a subpath without a move
and has no vertex of its own.

The directions are the stroke expansion's (SG5), read by the same code: the start direction of a segment is
`P_k - P_0` for the least `k` with `P_k != P_0`, the end direction `P_n - P_k` for the greatest with `P_k != P_n`, and a
segment with neither has no extent. The rule for a segment without extent is that it is looked past on both sides: the
incoming direction of a vertex is that of the nearest segment with extent at or before it in its subpath, the outgoing
that of the nearest after it, so a run of zero-length segments belongs to neither the vertices around it nor the corner
they sit at. A closed subpath is read round: what lies before its first segment with extent is its last, and after its
last is its first, which gives the start's corner (the move's vertex and the close's, one point) the closing line's end
direction coming in and the first segment's start direction going out, and does the same for any vertex inside a run of
zero-length segments at the start or end.

The marker direction is the outgoing direction at a vertex with no incoming one, the incoming at one with no outgoing, the
bisector at one with both, and `(1, 0)` at one with neither. The bisector is the unit vector of the sum of the two unit
directions, and at an exact reversal, where the sum is zero, the incoming direction turned by `gshgeom_normal_right`
(SVG leaves the side open; this is the one a browser takes for a reversal along +x). The angle is the caller's:
`gmath_atan2` of the components is bit-exact on every target.

The unit vector divides by the length computed after scaling by the larger coordinate, so a vector of 1e-300 or 1e99 is as
good as any other. Everything is `+ - * /` and one square root (`gmath_sqrt`), computed without contraction, so a vertex
has the same bits on every target: the cross-target probe hashes 500 random paths (a coarse grid, where zero-length
segments, reversals and closed corners are common, and a fine one, where the bisectors are irrational) as the committed
`vertex-hash`. The walk allocates nothing and is linear in the elements: the search for the next segment with extent only
moves forward.

## Arcs

`arc.h`. A 2D model path can hold an elliptical arc as one element (verb `GSHGEOM_VERB_ARC`, 7). An SVG `A`, a Canvas
`arc`, a DXF `ARC` or a G-code `G2` are each one arc, so a writer can say `<circle>` and a toolpath sink `G2` instead of
recovering a circle from control points and weights. The rules are numbered AR1 to AR18 (the record, its constructors,
the decomposition and what every reader does with an arc) and CV1 to CV6 (the conic route of rational curves, which an
arc's pieces take); the tests, the oracle and the plants cite them.

### The element (AR1 to AR4)

An arc keeps its **start and end exactly as the caller gave them** and its centre form: a centre `c`, two conjugate
half-diameters `u` and `v`, a start parameter `t0`, a signed `sweep` and the angle unit the two are in. The curve is
`P(t) = c + u cos t + v sin t` for `t` from `t0` to `t0 + sweep`. The conjugate form is closed under every affine map (the
map is applied to the three vectors; no singular value decomposition, whose radii would be irrational), and the radii
and rotation of the SVG and Canvas forms are derived, never stored. The unit is stored (radians, degrees, gradians or
turns) so that a quarter turn in the three exact units stays exact through the decomposition.

A record is accepted in this order, a failed call leaving the path as it was: `ERR_INVALID` for a NULL or 3D path, no
current point (the arc's start is the current point), a non-finite number, an unknown unit or flag bit, `cross(u, v) = 0`
(the ellipse must have area, decided exactly with the orientation predicate) or a sweep of zero or more than one turn; then
`ERR_LIMIT` for a coordinate or half-diameter past the coordinate limit, `|t0|` past the rotation map's quarter-turn
limit, or the element limit; then `ERR_INVALID` if the start is not the current point to the bit or the centre form
misses an end; then `ERR_LIMIT` again if a piece of the decomposition cannot be made (the meeting of two tangents can lie
past the coordinate limit when the centre form is near it, and an arc that cannot be decomposed could not be read by anything
that walks a path by its pieces); then `ERR_OOM`.

The ends are authoritative and the centre form must agree with them: `P(t0)` and `P(t0 + sweep)` must be within
`GSHGEOM_ARC_AGREE` (`2^-40`) times the arc's scale of the stored start and end, the scale being the largest magnitude of
any coordinate of the start, end and centre and of `|u|_inf + |v|_inf`. That is a ceiling chosen to be provable for every
path through the code, not a target: the tests measure the worst disagreement of the constructors at least eight times
below it, and assert the bound at the bound and one past it. A sweep of exactly one turn requires `end = start` to the bit,
so a closed subpath of arcs closes to the bit. The direction of an arc is `sign(sweep) * sign(cross(u, v))`, exact; every
constructor builds `cross(u, v) > 0`, so a positive sweep runs from +x toward +y (SVG's sweep flag 1), and only a mapped
arc under a mirror has `cross(u, v) < 0`. The sine and cosine of a parameter come from `gshgeom_sincos`, the quarter-turn
reduction the rotation map uses: in degrees, gradians and turns a multiple of a quarter turn is exactly 0 and +-1.

### The constructors (AR5 to AR7)

**The endpoint form** (`gshgeom_path_arc_endpoint_to`; SVG 2, F.6.5 and F.6.6) takes the end, `rx`, `ry`, the rotation, and
the two flags, each exactly 0 or 1. Equal ends add nothing (`NOTHING`); a zero radius adds a line (`LINE`); otherwise
the radii are made absolute and, if they are too small, scaled up so that the arc is the half ellipse with the centre at
the chord's midpoint. Whether they are too small is decided on exact sums (`src/exact`, as `orient2d` is), not on rounded
ones; and the radicand `1 - |m|^2` that places the centre is evaluated exactly and rounded once, so the centre keeps its
accuracy as the ends approach a half ellipse, where a radicand computed from rounded sums loses half its digits. The sweep
is not a difference of two arctangents (which cannot show a sweep below the spacing of the start angle) but twice
`atan2(sqrt(a^2 + b^2), sqrt(N))` of the two exact sums, accurate however small the chord is. The `CIRCULAR` flag is set
iff the radii are bit equal.

**The centre form** (`gshgeom_path_arc_center_to` for Canvas `arc` and `ellipse`, DXF `ARC`, `CIRCLE`; and
`gshgeom_path_arc_conjugate_to` for the record's own fields, DXF `ELLIPSE` giving a major axis vector and a ratio). With a
current point the arc starts there; without one a subpath begins at `P(t0)`. An explicit end overrides `P(t0 + sweep)`
(G-code names it). A sweep of exactly one turn closes: `end = start` to the bit, which Canvas's
`arc(x, y, r, 0, 2 pi)` needs, since `cos(2 pi)` is not 1. `gshgeom_arc_point` evaluates `P(t)` as the constructor does, so a
front-end that needs Canvas's connecting line can call `line_to` with the bits the arc starts from.

**The tangent forms.** `gshgeom_path_arc_tangent_to` is Canvas `arcTo` exactly (a line when the points coincide, the
radius is zero or the three are collinear by the exact predicate; otherwise a line to the first tangent point, unless it is
the current point, and a circular arc to the second), and `gshgeom_path_arc_bulge_to` is a DXF polyline bulge
(`bulge = tan(sweep / 4)`). Both keep their computed ends authoritative and check the agreement like any other record.

### The decomposition (AR8)

`gshgeom_arc_pieces` gives the arc as at most four rational quadratics of at most a quarter turn each (1 to 4 pieces for
a sweep up to one, two, three and four quarter turns, the comparison exact). Piece `k` runs from `t0 + (k sweep) / n` to the
next; its weights are `(1, w, 1)` with `w = cos h` for its half sweep `h`, **exactly `gmath_sqrt(0.5)` for a quarter turn**; its
control point is the meeting of the tangents at its two ends, and where both tangents are parallel to an axis (an exact
zero component) each coordinate is taken from the end point on that tangent, so a rounded rectangle's corner has the control
point `(E.x, S.y)` exactly and the first and last directions of the arc are bit-exact axis vectors. The first piece starts at
the arc's `start` and the last ends at its `end`. The pieces lie on the ellipse of the centre form to within
`GSHGEOM_ARC_DECOMP` (`2^-44`) times the scale, a limit the tests assert at its bound and one past it. Every operation is one
`double` operation in the written order, with no contraction.

### What reads an arc (AR9 to AR16)

An arc is **one segment** of its subpath. It has extent when some control point of its pieces differs from the first; its
start direction is the first such piece's and its end direction the last such piece's, read by the code the stroke expansion
reads them with. **A boundary between two of an arc's pieces is not a vertex**: not a join, a cap, a marker position or a
corner for any reader. The table says what each operation does.

| Operation | With an arc |
|---|---|
| `gshgeom_path_element` | `ERR_UNSUPPORTED`: a reader that predates arcs fails instead of drawing the chord. `gshgeom_path_verb` and `gshgeom_path_arc` read one back; `gshgeom_path_lower_arcs` gives the path with each arc replaced by its pieces. |
| copy, equal, hash, dump | The record, bit for bit (`-0` is not `+0`). Hash after the verb byte 7: flags, unit, then the twelve doubles of start, end, centre, `u`, `v`, `t0`, `sweep`; the dump line is `A <flags> <unit>` and the twelve bit patterns. A path with no arc hashes as it always did. |
| bounds | The union of the exact boxes of the pieces, so includes both ends. |
| `gshgeom_arc_map` | The ends and the centre as points and the half-diameters as vectors, so the result is the map's image to the rounding of the one composer; `unit`, `t0` and `sweep` are kept. A mirror flips the direction. `CIRCULAR` is kept if and only if the map's kind tag has none of `SCALES_NONUNIFORM`, `SKEWS`, `SINGULAR`, `GENERAL`; a rotation by an arbitrary angle leaves `u . v` a few ulps from zero, so the tag, never a test of the numbers, is the predicate. A singular map is `ERR_INVALID`; the device route maps the pieces instead. `gshgeom_arc_circle` answers a flagged arc's centre, radius and sense. |
| measurement | The pieces, each as a rational quadratic, in order; `segment_count` counts pieces and the frame names the element and the piece. |
| stroke, dashes | The body is the union of the pieces' bodies; joins and caps are made at the arc's two ends only, from its end directions and (for the `arcs` join) the curvature there; no join at a piece boundary. A dash end inside an arc cuts a piece, and the dash is stroked as the cut conics. A stroke of an arc and of its pieces written as rational quadratics have the same outline hash where the pieces are tangent continuous to the bit and a join emits nothing at a zero turn (butt, bevel); for the other joins the covered regions agree within the stroke tolerance. |
| vertices | One vertex per arc, at its authoritative end. A rounded rectangle's corner has bit-equal incoming and outgoing directions, so the marker angle there is exact. A full-turn arc has both directions at its vertex; a zero-extent arc is looked past, like any segment without extent. |
| curve outline | The pieces, each lowered as a rational quadratic is. |
| device path, flattening | The pieces through the conic route (CV1 to CV6, below); the device ends are the arc's own ends rounded once, so a closed arc closes in device space. |
| limits | An arc is one element for `max_elements`; its device path counts its elements and points against the device path's limits (`ERR_LIMIT`, nothing truncated). |

### The conic route (CV1 to CV6)

The device path of a model path under a map treats every rational curve (a rational quadratic, a rational cubic and each
piece of an arc) by one route, part of the flattener's definition. The control points are mapped in `double`
(`gshgeom_map_apply_point`), scaled by 64 (exact), and the weights are unchanged (an affine map keeps them). The homogeneous
curve `(w X, w Y, w)` is split at the parameter 1/2 by de Casteljau until **every interior control point of the projected
piece lies within `t - 1` units of the piece's chord**, when the piece becomes a line to its end, rounded once to 26.6. `t`
is the flattener's tolerance (at least `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_MIN_TOLERANCE`; `gshgeom_device_path_from_model_with_tolerance`
takes it, `gshgeom_device_path_from_model` uses `GSHGEOM_FLATTEN_TOLERANCE`). The ends of the whole curve are the mapped
control points themselves, rounded once. A split point is the quotient of its homogeneous point clamped into the box of the
control points; the pieces are visited left to right; a piece deeper than the cap is ended as it stands. Everything is one
`double` operation in the written order, no `long double`, no fused multiply-add, no library function, so the same bits on
every target.

**The guarantee.** For control points that each round into `int32` and weights in `[2^-100, 2^100]` (the range a path
accepts), every point of the exact curve is within `t - 0.29` units of the emitted polyline, and the depth cap
(`GSHGEOM_CONIC_MAX_DEPTH`, 240) never ends a piece that is not flat. The proof is stated above the code in
`src/device/device.c`; in outline: (a) a rational Bezier curve with positive weights lies in the convex hull of its projected
control points, and so does each piece, whose control points de Casteljau gives; (b) the `double` homogeneous halves are within
`2^-10` unit of the exact ones at every depth up to the cap (the rounding of an average adds at most `u` relative, and the
error grows by at most three per level); (c) the squared distance test is within `2^-16` unit of the true distance; (d) the
rounding of the vertices moves a chord by at most 0.7072; so the hull of a flat piece lies within `(t - 1) + 2^-16 + 2^-10 +
0.7072 = t - 0.2918` of the emitted segment; (e) the cap never binds: neighbouring homogeneous points of a piece of parameter
length `2^-k` differ by at most `2^-k` times those of the whole, which bounds a control point's distance from the chord by
`2^235.1 * 2^-k` units, below 0.04 at `k = 240`. A weight ratio of `2^200`, which a path accepts, is what needs 236 levels rather
than the twenty of an ordinary curve (the integer flattener needs 20 for the `int32` range): a uniform split of such a curve
spends about a hundred levels before it divides the curve's length at all, each emitting one line, the price of an input no
one writes. `tests/unit/test_conic.cpp` checks the proof against dense exact evaluation of the curves (the Bernstein form in
`long double` at dyadic parameters, thirty-three inside every piece the route made, and `2^-k` and `1 - 2^-k` for every depth
the proof allows) on every size class up to the whole `int32` range, and a variant of the route with a smaller cap shows that
a cap that binds is seen.

**Hostile input** (CV6): control points at the map limit, a weight at `2^100` or `2^-100`, and a piece that does not flatten
before the device range is exceeded are `ERR_LIMIT` for a coordinate that does not round into `int32`, never a hang and never
a depth that binds. The cross-target value over random conics, arcs of every sweep and tiny and huge scales is the
`conic-hash` pair of `tests/corpus/geometry.hash`; the arc constructors and pieces are the `arc-hash` pair and the vertices of
paths that hold arcs the `arc-vertex-hash` pair.

### What checks it

`tools/arc-oracle.py` states the endpoint, centre and tangent forms, the bulge and the decomposition in exact rationals and
130-digit decimals with no code shared with the library, and `tools/gen-arc-vectors.py` writes `tests/arc_vectors.h` from it:
every flag pair, the radius corrections, rotations in every unit, the near-semicircle on either side of one at three scales,
ends one ulp apart and the quarter-aligned arcs a rounded rectangle uses. The oracle and the library are both written from
the same text, so they could agree and be wrong about which of the four arcs a flag pair means. The check against a
renderer's behaviour (the four-arc minimal pair, and an elongated rotated ellipse, since a rotation sign error is invisible on
a circle) is not part of `make test`.

## Named primitives

`shapes.h` builds the three shapes every front-end draws, so that each gets the same elements: a rectangle, a rounded rectangle and an
ellipse. They are ordinary path elements (lines and quarter-turn arcs); a path carries no tag. Which shape the front-end meant is
a fact kept above the path, where it cannot outlive an edit (the scene library's primitive tag), and the geometry here is what a
sink that ignores the tag draws, so it is defined exactly. The rules keep the names the header and the tests cite.

**PR2 What is valid.** Decided in this order, `ERR_INVALID` unless stated, a failed call leaving the path as it was: a NULL or 3D
path; a flip that is not 0 or 1; a non-finite number; a negative width, height or radius component; for the ellipse, a radius that is
not positive; for the rounded rectangle, an overlap of the corner curves on a side; then `ERR_LIMIT` for any coordinate the builder
derives past `GSHGEOM_COORD_LIMIT` (`x + w`, `cx + rx`, each correctly rounded) or the path's element limit; then `ERR_OOM`. A corner
with a zero radius component is square and counts as `{0, 0}`; the overlap rule sees the canonical radii, so a square corner
never overlaps anything. The overlap is decided **exactly**: `r[0].rx + r[1].rx > w` (the top), `r[1].ry + r[2].ry > h` (the right),
`r[2].rx + r[3].rx > w` (the bottom) and `r[3].ry + r[0].ry > h` (the left), each the sum of two doubles compared with a double in the
reals. Equality is valid (a stadium, a circle). The sum `0.1 + 0.9` is `1.0` in `double` and a little over one in the reals, so
a box of width `1` is refused for those radii and a box one ulp wider is not. The waist does not clamp or scale radii: each format has
its own rule (SVG clamps each radius to half its side, CSS and Canvas scale all corners by one factor) and the front-end applies it.

**Coordinates.** Every coordinate is the correctly rounded value of the real expression named: `x + w`, `x + w - r`, `cx - rx`, each a
sum rounded once from the exact sum. A sum with a zero term is the other term bit for bit (`x + 0` is `x`, so a point at `-0` stays), and
a sum that is exactly zero is `+0`.

**PR3 Rect.** `M (x, y)`, `L (x + w, y)`, `L (x + w, y + h)`, `L (x, y + h)`, `Z`: SVG 2's `rect` and Canvas `rect` with a
non-negative size: the top edge left to right, then down the right side and back: the positive direction (AD-6; clockwise on a y-down
screen). A zero width or height is allowed and gives coincident points (Canvas strokes such a rect as a line; SVG's "disable
rendering" is the front-end's decision to build nothing).

**PR4 Rounded rect.** Radii `{rx, ry}` in the order top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left. The path starts after the top-left
radius: `M (x + rTL.rx, y)`, `L (x + w - rTR.rx, y)`, the top-right arc (if that corner is round) to `(x + w, y + rTR.ry)`,
`L (x + w, y + h - rBR.ry)`, the bottom-right arc to `(x + w - rBR.rx, y + h)`, `L (x + rBL.rx, y + h)`, the bottom-left arc to
`(x, y + h - rBL.ry)`, `L (x, y + rTL.ry)`, the top-left arc to the start, `Z`. The four lines are **always** emitted, even when a side's
radii sum to the side and the line has no length: the structure is a function of which corners are round and nothing else. Each arc
is a positive quarter turn in degrees (exactly representable, so the control polygon of a quarter-aligned corner is exact, AR8), with
the centre `C`, the half-diameters `u = (rx, 0)` and `v = (0, ry)` and `t0` of `-90`, `0`, `90`, `180` for the corners in that order; its
start and end are the named points to the bit, so the subpath closes to the bit. It is circular iff `rx` and `ry` are bit equal. With no
round corner the path is PR3's.

**PR5 Ellipse.** `M (cx + rx, cy)`, then four arcs about `(cx, cy)` with `u = (rx, 0)`, `v = (0, ry)`, in degrees, sweep `+90`, `t0` of
0, 90, 180, 270, ending at `(cx, cy + ry)`, `(cx - rx, cy)`, `(cx, cy - ry)` and the start, then `Z`: SVG 2's equivalent path for
`ellipse` and `circle` (four quarter arcs from the "3 o'clock" point, clockwise). Circular iff `rx` and `ry` are bit equal.

**PR13 The flips.** `flip_x` and `flip_y` (each 0 or 1) mirror the shape in the box's vertical and horizontal axis, the start corner
and the turn following the mirror. They are the four sign cases of Canvas `rect(x, y, w, h)`: no flip is `w >= 0, h >= 0`, `flip_x` is `w < 0`,
`flip_y` is `h < 0`, both is both negative. The mirror is one exchange: write each coordinate as a distance from the left edge (`x + a`) or
the right (`x + w - a`), and from the top (`y + b`) or the bottom (`y + h - b`); `flip_x` swaps left with right and `flip_y` top with
bottom. The elements, their order and the closing are the unflipped shape's. Every coordinate is rounded once after the exchange, never a mirror of a
rounded coordinate.

| flips | rect starts at | on screen | ellipse starts at | on screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | top-left | clockwise | angle 0 (`cx + rx`) | clockwise |
| `flip_x` | top-right | counter-clockwise | angle pi (`cx - rx`) | counter-clockwise |
| `flip_y` | bottom-left | counter-clockwise | angle 0 | counter-clockwise |
| both | bottom-right | clockwise | angle pi | clockwise |

The direction is the parity of the flips. For the ellipse `flip_x` negates `u` and `flip_y` negates `v`, with `t0` and the sweep unchanged,
so the arcs' orientation (AR3) is negative exactly when one flip is set. SVG uses no flip. A front-end maps Canvas `rect(x, y, w, h)` to the
box `(x + min(w, 0), y + min(h, 0), |w|, |h|)` with `flip_x` iff `w < 0` and `flip_y` iff `h < 0`, and builds no untagged path for any sign case.

**The rounded rectangle's flips are not the rect's.** Canvas `roundRect` with a negative width or height does not mirror the path as `rect`
does, and the HTML Standard is silent: its `roundRect` steps carry no negative-size case, and read literally for a negative width they
start outside the box (`(x + upperLeft.x, y)` with `x` the right edge). What Chromium draws, measured (below), is what the builder does.
The box is normalized (`(x + min(w, 0), y + min(h, 0), abs(w), abs(h))`) and then two things happen. (1) The radii go to the corners the
mirror gives them: the list is in the order the sign case visits the corners, `r[0]` going to the corner `rect` would start at, so the
box's corners take top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left from `r` as `0 1 2 3` (no flip), `1 0 3 2` (`flip_x`), `3 2 1 0` (`flip_y`)
and `2 3 0 1` (both). (2) The path is the clockwise path of the box (PR4: it starts after the box's top-left radius) when the flips are
equal, and the **reversal** of it when they differ: the same start `(x + tl.rx, y)`, then the top-left arc and the left edge, the bottom
edge, the right edge up and the top-right arc, each arc run the other way (sweep -90, the half-diameters unchanged), the four lines
always there (`M`, an arc or a line, a line, ..., a closing line to the start, `Z`). Two flips are therefore the unflipped path with
permuted radii, and one flip a path no mirror of `rect` gives. A flipped rounded rectangle with no round corner keeps its four lines
and is a rounded rectangle, never a rect. Firefox 153 agrees for an even number of flips and for the radii, and starts an odd one at
`(x + w - tr.rx, y)` (the other end of the top edge), still counter-clockwise: the two engines differ and the waist follows Chromium.

**What was checked against a browser.** `tools/` does not run a browser; the minimal pairs were run by hand, dashing a 2 px stroke with a dash of
4 to 40 px and a gap of 100000 so that the first dash marks where the path starts and which way it turns. In headless Chrome 156 (Chromium):
Canvas `rect` with each of the four sign cases, Canvas `ellipse` from angle 0 and pi both ways round (a full turn), and SVG `<rect>`,
`<rect rx ry>`, `<ellipse>`, `<circle>` all start and turn as the table and PR3 to PR5 say; the HTML Standard's `rect` and `ellipse` steps
say the same. In Firefox 153 (ESR, headless): Canvas `rect` and `ellipse` give the same boxes. For `roundRect`, a page draws the model above
and the browser's own `roundRect` for each sign case and five radii sets (circular, elliptical, all zero, one corner, two) and compares the
whole canvas after a fill and six dashed strokes: Chromium's equals the model in all twenty cases, and Firefox's in all but the odd
parities (above). The SVG elements were not run in Firefox, and Safari was not run (no WebKit browser here).

**What checks it.** `tools/gen-shapes-vectors.py` states PR2 to PR5 and PR13 in exact rationals (`fractions.Fraction`, rounded once),
sharing no code with the library, and writes `tests/shapes_vectors.h`: boxes with a zero side, a negative zero, a sum that rounds, the
coordinate limit and one ulp past it, by the four flips for the rect and the ellipse; rounded rectangles with one to four round corners,
elliptical corners and radii summing exactly to a side; the overlap table decided on exact sums; and the refusals. The test compares every
element bit for bit, reads each arc's piece back and requires the control polygon to be the corner of the box, and checks the refusals
leave the path as it was. The cross-target value over random boxes, radii and flips is the `shapes-hash` pair of
`tests/corpus/geometry.hash`.

## Strokes

`stroke.h` states what a stroke covers, once, as rules SG1 to SG18, and
`gshgeom_stroke_expand` turns a centerline, the parameters and a map into an
outline whose nonzero fill is that region within the tolerance. The rules are
the normative text; this section records where they come from, the readings of
the texts they follow, the construction and its proof in outline, and the
revision.

### Sources

The rules follow SVG 2 and the WHATWG HTML standard's canvas line styles, each
pinned (`tools/svg2-texts/fetch.sh` fetches them and checks their SHA-256): the
SVG 2 Candidate Recommendation of 2018-10-04 (its painting and paths chapters),
the SVG 2 editor's draft at `w3c/svgwg` commit `f846f12e3f50`, the SVG Strokes
module draft at the same commit, and the WHATWG HTML source at commit
`c6c3055c1287`. The editor's draft has withdrawn the `miter-clip` and `arcs`
values of `stroke-linejoin`; the 2018 Candidate Recommendation is the one text
that defines them, and SG8 follows it.

Where the texts leave a point open, the rules settle it by the limit of the
cases they define, and say so:

| Point | Reading |
|---|---|
| The body's half width | The CR's stroke-shape algorithm says "within distance stroke-width" of the centerline, where its caps and joins use half the width: half the width (SG6), the erratum read as every renderer reads it. |
| A cusp inside a segment | The literal union of normals sweeps nothing at an exact cusp; every nearby curve's normals sweep a half turn there. SG6 adds the disc, which keeps the region continuous in the control points. |
| Miter-clip's clip distance | The CR's property text: half the miter limit times the width from the join point. One sentence of its miter-clip description says "half the value of miter length"; read as the same distance. |
| A reversal (`phi = pi`) | Each join's limit as the turn approaches a half turn: nothing for bevel and miter, the half disc ahead for round, the rectangle of length `L h` for miter-clip. Arcs' two one-sided limits differ (each pairs a different segment's curvature with a side), so it is their union. |
| Arcs: the curvature of an end | The CR gives it for quadratic and cubic Beziers and elliptical arcs; SG8 uses the curvature of the segment at that end for every kind (for a rational kind with the weights' factor `w_0 w_2 / w_1^2`), which is what the CR's formulas are. |
| Arcs: "curvature greater than 2 / stroke-width" | The path's curvature magnitude, `|kappa| > 1 / h`, strict, decided exactly: the radius at which an offset edge degenerates, and the reading that does not depend on the direction of travel. |
| Arcs: a vanishing first difference | The CR says the curvature is infinite and a line is used: a line, whose curvature counts as zero for the fallbacks. |
| Arcs: which meeting point | The closest to the vertex that is on both extensions. An extension that is a line is a ray (forward from the incoming edge's end along its direction, backward from the outgoing edge's start), so a meeting point behind its start is not on it; a circle is all of itself. Of two candidates the closer is the one on the vertex's side of the line of centres (a circle and a line: nearer the vertex's foot along the line), a comparison that needs no distances; they tie exactly when the vertex is on that line, which is a reversal of two curved segments, and the tie goes to the one reached first along the incoming edge's extension. |
| Arcs: the radius adjustment | The least positive amount that makes the circles touch; a circle and a line: the circle alone grows (the limit of both circle cases as one radius tends to infinity); where nothing can touch (parallel tangents), the CR's rectangle. |
| Arcs: the region | The bevel together with what the closed boundary V, A, the first extension to the meeting point, the second extension back to B, V encloses by nonzero winding: the region the CR's lines and arcs enclose, read literally. Where the extensions curl past the vertex (a reversal of two curved segments) that region is not star-shaped from the vertex, and a union of segments from the vertex would cover a far larger area than the CR's boundary encloses. |
| Arcs: the clip | The clip is applied to the boundary: its parts in the half plane, closed along the clip line, enclose exactly the region cut by the half plane (a closed curve and its clipping wind the same about every point of the half plane). `Gamma` is the circle tangent to the bisector at the vertex through the meeting point (a line when the point is on the bisector); the clip line is perpendicular to `Gamma` at arc length `L h`, keeping the vertex's side. A meeting point behind the vertex on the bisector takes the straight clip. |
| Arcs under device width | The curvatures of the mapped curve: the CR's non-scaling-stroke clause computes the whole outline in host space. |
| A degenerate square's direction | SVG 2's effective tangent, read literally across subpaths: the end direction of the last segment with extent before it in the path, else the start direction of the first after it, else local `+x`. |
| Canvas | One-point subpaths are dropped and zero-length segments pruned: a canvas front-end lowers to "omit". |

### The construction

The region is a union of pieces: the body of a stretch of a segment, a join, a
cap, a degenerate shape. Each piece is emitted as the convex hull of a few core
points it computes, inflated by an octagon and rounded to 26.6, as positively
wound polygons (src/stroke/emit.c). A piece states two bounds: how far the
region can be outside its core hull (`d_in`) and how far the core hull can be
outside the region (`d_out`). The inflation is `d_in` plus the rounding's reach,
so after the rounding the polygon still contains its piece; the outward reach is
`d_out + 1.0826 (d_in + rho) + rho`, `rho` the rounding and arithmetic allowance,
and a piece is emitted only when that is within `tau - 1/4` units. The outline
therefore contains the region and lies within `tau - 1/4` units of it, and no
polygon winds negatively, so overlaps never cancel (SG13).

Lines, joins, caps and degenerate shapes are polygons of their own corners, or
circular sectors drawn as the control polygons of halved rational quadratic arcs
(no angle is formed). A curve segment is split by de Casteljau at the parameter
7/16 until each piece is a **band** (both cones of its hodograph narrower than a
quarter turn, its curvature bound times `h` below the cosine of the cone's width,
so no two normals meet and each rail is a graph over its chord within
`(lambda / 2) sin(alpha)`) or a **fan** (a short piece: the double sector of its
normal cone about its start, or a disc when its end normals are nearly opposite,
which is how a cusp gets its disc). Errors of the doubles are carried with every
coefficient. A piece still refused after 64 splits is `ERR_LIMIT`, never drawn
coarser. The arcs join is built from its boundary: the straight edges and the
circumscribed polylines of the extensions' quarter-turn sub-arcs (each within
the allowed distance of its arc), cut by the clip's half plane (the boundary's
parts inside it, with the gaps closed along the clip line, where chords that run
both ways over the same stretch cancel), then cut into slabs at every height an
edge ends or two edges cross, and each stretch of nonzero winding between
neighbouring edges is one trapezoid. Every piece is positively wound; the
boundary may have at most 2048 edges, past which the join is `ERR_LIMIT`. The
arcs join's clip is the one place `math`'s trigonometry is used.

The proof is above the code: emit.c (the theorem and the rounding), body.c (bands
and fans), join.c (SG9's exact decision and the miter tip's error), arc.c (arcs
and the arcs join). Its arithmetic bound assumes every magnitude is within
`2^30` pixels (anchors, the mapped control points' offsets, the half width and
the miter tip); a larger one is `ERR_LIMIT`.

### What checks it

`tests/stroke_oracle.h` decides membership from the rules alone, in
`long double`, sharing no code with the expansion, and samples a superset of the
region's boundary at 1/256 pixel. `tests/unit/test_stroke_oracle.cpp` checks it
first where the region is the set of points within `h` of the centerline (round
joins and caps), sees it fail with a sector on the inner side, and checks it
against the exact verdicts of `tools/gen-stroke-vectors.py` (an independent model
in mpmath, run in the pinned oracle image). `tests/unit/test_stroke_matrix.cpp`
compares the outline with the oracle at every decided point over the case matrix,
checks that no polygon winds negatively and every vertex is inside the
conservative stroke box plus the tolerance, and records the deviation profile.
`tests/unit/test_stroke.cpp` covers the parameters, statuses, parts, hash, dump,
limits and allocation failure.

### Dashes

A dash array cuts the centerline before the body is built (`stroke.h`, SG19 to
SG30). Each dash is an open piece of the centerline and is stroked by the rules
of the solid stroke (SG5 to SG10), so a dashed stroke is the same union of
positively wound pieces and keeps the same tolerance proof; only the ends of
each piece and the vertices inside it are new. `src/stroke/dash.c` does it.

**The distance and the cut.** Lengths are measured with `along.h` at its finest
tolerance (`2^-46`), in the width space: the model path under local width, the
mapped path under device width (the expansion rebuilds the centerline element for
element under the map and measures that). A dash end at a distance becomes a
segment and a parameter through the measured table (a boundary is the later
segment's, the end of a subpath the last segment's); the piece of a segment
between two parameters is cut by de Casteljau in homogeneous coordinates, the
outer end points copied bit for bit, so a piece that keeps an end of its segment
keeps that vertex exactly. The join at a vertex strictly inside a dash is decided
from the two original segments that meet there (SG22), never from the cut
pieces, whose computed control points would show a smooth vertex as a tiny turn;
a cap's direction at a cut is the hodograph of the original segment at the
parameter (`N'D - ND'` for a rational kind), or SG5's exact end direction when the
cut is an end of the segment. A dot (SG24) takes the same direction.

**The positions** (SG21) are SVG 2's "dash positions", with a running position
replaced by `(q P + S) - o` so that the error of an end does not grow with the
dashes before it, and the phase reduced by exact long division (no quotient in
doubles). One tie differs from the CR's loop and is the only difference: the CR
takes the least index whose prefix sum is at least the phase (`>=`), so a phase
exactly at the end of a positive dash leaves a dash of no length at the start of
the path, which round and square caps draw as a dot. Every shipping renderer draws
nothing there, and the literal rule leaves a valid DXF linetype that begins with a
space unlowerable (the line would start exactly at the end of the dash before it).
A positive dash that the phase has used up entirely is therefore over; a dash of no
length exactly at the phase is kept, and a dash that would begin exactly at the end
of a subpath is not made (the CR's `while position < pathlength`), so the
convention is half open at both ends.

| Point | Reading |
|---|---|
| A closed subpath | The CR defines each dash as a pair of distances within the subpath, with its own caps, and no join at the start of a closed subpath. A dash that crosses the start point is read as one piece (the limit of the defined cases: a dash that nothing cuts stays whole): when the first piece began before the path and the last dash would go on past its end they are one dash with a join at the start vertex and caps only at its two ends. A dash that ends exactly at the start, or begins exactly there, stays two, each with its cap; a dash that would cover the whole subpath is the closed stroke. |
| A zero-length dash | Two caps back to back (the text's start cap and end cap at one position; Canvas's "two caps placed back-to-back"): a disc, a square along the tangent, nothing for butt. It is drawn whatever `degenerate` says (Canvas omits zero-length subpaths and draws dotted lines). |
| The tangent of a dot | The right-hand limit, as along.h's boundary rule: the segment that begins at the point; at the end of the subpath the last segment's end direction. |
| One pattern for the path | The pattern runs over the path distance and is cut at the end of each subpath (a dash does not cross the jump of a move); the phase at a subpath is the offset plus the distance before it. |
| A degenerate subpath | The first piece of SG21 for a length of zero: a dot at its point when the pattern is on there, with SG11's effective tangent; omitted when `degenerate` says omit. |
| Alignment | The clip region is of the undashed closed subpaths; the aligned region is the dashed centered region at twice the width. |

**Dash steps** (`GSHGEOM_Limits.max_dash_steps`). An entry of the pattern is a step, whether or not its dash emits anything, and a
step costs a fraction of a microsecond (about 0.3, and a dot with a butt cap emits nothing), so a short pattern on a long path makes
tens of millions of steps and seconds of work with no output for any other cap to refuse. The expansion visits at most
`max_dash_steps` of them over all of a stroke's subpaths; 0 is the derived default `4 max_points + 4096`, so a caller who states
nothing meets what it always met, and one who wants less states it. A stroke that would visit more is `ERR_LIMIT`. The refusal is
made **before the walk** wherever the lengths show it: a subpath of length `L` holds at least `n (floor(L / P) - 1)` steps after its
first piece (`n` entries a period `P`; the phase leaves at most one period out, and every entry of a period that ends inside the
subpath starts inside it), so a stroke whose subpaths promise more than the cap is refused at once, as the walk would have refused it,
and the walk is made only for a stroke that may pass. It is a function of the stroke alone, not of what is drawn, so refusal does not
depend on damage and a cached outline is unaffected. The promise is a lower bound and never refuses a stroke the walk accepts (a
test finds the least cap that passes by bisection and requires it at or above the promise, and for one subpath equal to the
pattern walk's own count). A walk of the section that is visible only, which the phase arithmetic would allow, is not done: it would
make the outline depend on the window.

**The cut position's error** (SG26). The error of a distance is `along.h`'s
contract (its estimate of a segment's error is at most the tolerance times the
control polygon's length; the running sum adds `rows 2^-53` of itself; the solve
inside a panel stops at `2^-52` of its length) plus the pattern's arithmetic; the
call refuses (`ERR_LIMIT`) unless that, times the map's norm, is at most `2^-20`
pixel. A dash end that moves by `delta` along the centerline moves every boundary
point of its end by at most `(1 + h kappa) delta`, so the cut costs at most
`(1 + h kappa) 2^-20` pixel of the outline's error, inside the reserve the
construction keeps of the tolerance wherever `h kappa <= 2^12`. The measurer's bound
is an estimate and not a proof; the tests compare the cuts with an independent
quadrature (1024 panels of 8-point Gauss-Legendre, long double) and the circle's
closed form.

**What checks it.** `tests/stroke_oracle.h` defines a dashed stroke from the text
alone (its own quadrature, the positions transcribed with a running position, cut
segments, dots, the dash across the start) and the matrix of
`tests/unit/test_stroke_matrix.cpp` compares the outline with it on lines, every
curve kind, closed and open subpaths, every join and cap, both scopes, both width
spaces and alignment. `tools/gen-dash-vectors.py` states the positions and the DXF
lowering in exact rationals; `test_dash.cpp` compares them bit for bit and checks
the dash across the start at exact points.

**DXF simple linetypes** (SG30, `gshgeom_stroke_dashes_from_dxf`). A pattern is a
list of elements (DXF group 49): a positive value is a dash with the pen down for
that length, a negative value a space, zero a dot. The sign convention is the
documented one for linetype definitions (for example the ezdxf linetype tutorial:
`> 0` a line, `< 0` a gap, `0` a dot; Autodesk's reference calls group 49 a "dash,
dot or space length"). The pattern repeats from the start of the line, so the last
run and the first are one run when they are of one kind, and the line starts inside
it: that is the offset. A pattern that begins with a space is lowered with the line
starting at the end of the dash before it (the half-open tie above). One that begins
with a space and ends with a dot has the dot of each period exactly where the line
starts, and the positions keep a dash of no length at the phase (the ordinary dotted
line, `[0, 10]` at offset 0, needs it), so its offset is the limit of the phases after
the dot instead: the next double above the dot's position in the array (for a lone dot,
the period times `2^-52`). That moves the start of the line by one unit in the last place
of that position, far inside the cut error the expansion already allows (SG26), and puts
the dot behind it; the pattern is otherwise exact, and no style field is needed. A
rule keyed to the array's first element, or to how the offset was reached, would draw
one periodic pattern differently depending on where the array was cut. Complex linetypes (embedded
shapes or text) are not lowered. The vectors are hand-built patterns that cover
every case of the merge and the rotation (dotted, dashed, dash-dot, divide, center,
phantom and border shapes, a space first, a dash last, a dot first, spaces and dots
only, a scale); the lowering is also checked by its definition (the pen-down
intervals of the raw pattern equal those of the lowered array under the positions).

### The revision

`GSHGEOM_STROKE_REVISION` and `gshgeom_stroke_revision()` number the
construction (1). The number is recorded where a rebaseline lives and is in no hash;
there is no selector for another revision.

The construction is the pieces above: octagon-inflated convex hulls, bands and fans split at 7/16, arcs by halved rational quadratics; within `tau - 1/4` units outward, containing the region. Dashes (SG19 to SG30) are part of it.

## Stroke curves

`stroke_curves.h` returns the region of `stroke.h` (SG6 to SG13) as a path of
lines, cubics and rational quadratics in target units, with a tolerance `t` stated
in those units (rules SG32 to SG43). The 26.6 polygon of `gshgeom_stroke_expand`
is what a rasterizer wants, its accuracy a sixteenth of the pixel of the map it
was given; a writer that meets a style field its format cannot express (inside and
outside alignment, a join or a dash scope the format lacks) must expand the stroke
and write the region as a fill, and a polygon accurate to the size it was expanded
for shows its facets when a viewer zooms. This outline is exact to a constant `t`
of the file's own unit, so a viewer at zoom `z` sees at most `t z`.

### What is exact and what is approximated

Lines offset to lines, circular arcs to concentric arcs, and every join and cap is
lines and circular arcs; an affine map takes a line to a line, a cubic to a cubic
and a rational quadratic to a rational quadratic with the same weights. So the
body of a line segment, a bevel, a miter, a miter-clip pentagon, a square cap and
a reversal's rectangle are polygons of lines, and a round join (the sector), a
round cap (the half disc), a degenerate disc and a reversal's half disc are lines
and rational-quadratic arcs of at most a quarter turn (middle weight
`cos(psi / 2) = sqrt((1 + p . q) / 2)`, the one square root of the construction;
an angle past a quarter turn is cut at the bisector). Under local width the pieces
are built in the centerline's space and mapped control point by control point, so
the elliptical pen of a non-uniform map is exact. Only the offsets of curved
segments are approximated.

### The offsets of a curved segment

A segment is split by de Casteljau at 7/16 (as the polygon expansion does) until
each piece is a **band**, a **fold** or a **fan**:

- A **band** is a piece whose normals do not meet within `h` (the hodograph's cone
  is a quarter turn and `kappa h < cos alpha`, exactly the polygon expansion's test).
  Its boundary is the two offset cubics, the cubic Hermite interpolants of
  `c(u) +- h n(u)` at the piece's ends, and the end normals. The interpolation
  remainder bounds the fit: `|O - C| <= (d^4 / 384) sup |O''''|` over a parameter
  interval of length `d` (the piece's own parameter, so `d = 1`). The supremum is
  certified: `G[j]` bounds `sup |c^(j)|` for `j <= 5` from the Bernstein form
  (`D c = N` gives `D c^(k) = N^(k) - sum_{i<k} C(k,i) D^(k-i) c^(i)` for a rational
  piece), `m` bounds `|c'|` from below (the cone's least projection over the largest
  weight squared), and with `s = |c'|`, `g = c'`, `T = c' / s`: from `s T = g`,
  `s T^(k) = g^(k) - sum_{i<k} C(k,i) s^(k-i) T^(i)`, and from `s^2 = g . g`,
  `2 s s^(k) = sum_{i<=k} C(k,i) g^(i) . g^(k-i) - sum_{0<i<k} C(k,i) s^(i) s^(k-i)`,
  so `|s^(k)| <= S[k]` and `|T^(k)| <= F[k]` term by term (`S[1] = |g'|`, since
  `|s'| = |g . g'| / |g|`; `F[1] = |g'| / m`, since `T' = g'_perp / s`), and
  `sup |O''''| <= G[4] + h F[4]`. Every bound is a sum of products of nonnegative
  numbers, raised by `1 + 2^-40` at each step for the rounding of its own
  computation. A piece is accepted when the fit is within the offset budget in
  target units (the model-space error times an upper bound of the map's norm), at
  most a quarter of `h`, the two fitted rails run forward along both end tangents
  at every control vector (so each is injective and never returns to an end
  normal's line), and their control hulls are apart (so they never meet): the loop
  is then a simple closed curve, and its orientation is the band's, since a move of
  at most `h / 4` along a boundary of length at most `4 length(c)` cannot change the
  sign of the band's area `2 h length(c)`.
- Consecutive bands of a segment are written as one contour: the left rails
  forward, the last end normal, the right rails backward, the first end normal.
  Their shared end normals cancel and are not written, and the point and tangent
  where two pieces meet are computed once, where the piece was split, and used by
  both.
- A **fold** is a piece whose band is not simple only because the curvature bound
  refuses (the offset folds, or comes within `h` of folding). The normals' sweep is
  the image of `F(u, lambda) = c(u) + lambda n(u)` over the piece times `[-h, h]`; the
  bilinear patch through the four ends of the two end normals is within
  `sup |F_uu| / 8 = (G[2] + h F[2]) / 8` of it, point for point, and that patch is
  the quadrilateral of the end normals, or two lobes (the triangles at the point
  where they cross) when they cross. It is written as positively wound triangles, so
  the nonzero fill is their union. The error is second order in the piece's
  extent however the offset folds, which is what keeps a tight bend of a wide stroke
  from costing the first power of the tolerance.
- A **fan** covers what no certificate reaches (a cusp, a stopped end), as the
  polygon expansion does: the sector of radius `h` over the cone of normals and
  its opposite, or the disc, down to the budget's extent. In curve mode it is
  inflated by its own displacement (`dm`, the distance of the piece's points from
  its start, in build space), the half planes of its sides moved out by `dm` with
  the circle of radius `h + dm` and a line `dm` behind the apex, so that it contains
  the piece and neighbouring fans overlap. Without that the fans abut and a crack
  of width `dm` opens between them.

### The tolerance and its budget

`t` is in `[2^-14, 2^4]` target units. The part's deviation from the region is at
most `t`: `t / 2` for offsets, folds, fans and the arcs join's polylines, `t / 4`
for the lowering of arcs to cubics (form `CUBIC` only), and a reserve of `t / 4`
for the double arithmetic (at most `t / 8`), the dash cut (SG26's gate keeps it
within `2^-20` target units, which is `t / 64` at the finest tolerance) and the
rest. The offsets get `t / 2` in **both** forms, so that form `CUBIC` is exactly
form `RATIONAL` passed through `gshgeom_stroke_curves_lower_cubic` at `t / 4`, bit
for bit. The arithmetic's reach is `2^-43` of the largest magnitude the
construction touches (a few hundred correctly rounded operations on quantities
under that magnitude), and it must be within `t / 8`: coordinates up to `t 2^40`
(and at most `2^30`), so up to `2^30` at the default tolerance and `2^26` at the
finest; a larger input is `GSHGEOM_ERR_LIMIT`, never a coarser curve.

### The lowering to cubics

A quadratic is raised exactly. A rational quadratic with ends of weight 1 and
middle weight `w < 1` (any rational quadratic has this form after a reparametrization,
`w = w_1 / sqrt(w_0 w_2)`) is an ellipse arc, the affine image `A` of the circular arc
of angle `psi = 2 acos w`. The cubic with the same ends and end tangents whose
handles are the fraction `4 w / (3 (1 + w))` of the control legs is, on the unit
circle with the arc centred on the x axis and handle `k = (4/3) tan(psi / 4)`:
`|Q(t)|^2 - 1` is a polynomial of degree 6 with a double root at `t = 0`, `1/2` and
`1` (the ends and the midpoint are on the circle with the circle's tangent), so
`|Q(t)|^2 - 1 = (3 k cos(psi/2) - 2 sin(psi/2))^2 t^2 (1-t)^2 (1-2t)^2`, whose
maximum is `1/108` of the square: the cubic stays outside the circle by
`e = sqrt(1 + (1-w)^3 / (27 (1+w))) - 1 <= (1-w)^3 / (54 (1+w))`, and being monotone
in angle it is within the same of the arc in the Hausdorff sense. Under an affine map
the bound is `|A| r` times that, with `|A|_F^2 r^2 = |P_2 - P_0|^2 / (4 s^2) + |P_1 - M|^2 w^2 / s^4`
from the control triangle (`s^2 = 1 - w^2`, `M` the chord's midpoint). Exact
bisection of the conic (a half has middle weight `sqrt((1 + w) / 2)`) cuts `e` by
about 64. The radial error of a quarter turn is `2.7e-4 r`, of 60 degrees `2.4e-5 r`,
of 45 degrees `4.2e-6 r`, of 30 degrees `3.7e-7 r`; it falls as `psi^6`. Any other
rational curve is bisected, in homogeneous coordinates, until `(w_max - w_min) / w_min`
times the diameter of its control polygon is within the tolerance (the polynomial
curve on the same control points differs from it by at most that).

### The arcs join, and what is not exact

The arcs join's closed boundary (the vertex, the first extension to the meeting
point, the second extension back, the vertex) is written as exact lines and arcs
when it is a polar graph about the vertex and the miter limit does not clip it: its
polar angle then runs one way along every side (a line has `cross(P, dP)` constant;
a circle `(z, r)` traversed in the sense `s` has `cross(P, dP) = s r (z . u + r)` at
`z + r u`, a cosine and a constant, extreme at the ends of a quarter arc or in the
direction of `z`), every point lies in the wedge between `A` and `B`, and a closed
curve that is a polar graph with its two radial sides is simple, of the orientation
of `cross(A, B)`. The bevel is added, since the region is its union with the
boundary's. Where the boundary curls (a reversal of two curved segments) or the clip
cuts it, the join is built as the polygon expansion builds it: straight edges and
circumscribed polylines of its sub-arcs of at most a quarter turn, cut into convex
trapezoids by the heights where an edge ends or two edges cross, within the offset
budget, and a join whose boundary needs more than 2048 edges (a very large circle
at a fine tolerance) is `GSHGEOM_ERR_LIMIT`.

A tight bend of a wide stroke is written as polygons in its fold, within `t / 2` of
the region and second order in the pieces' extent. The cost of a fold grows as the
square root of `1 / t`, and a cusp, which only a fan reaches, as `1 / t`.

The conservative box of a stroke (the path's box outset by `L h`) is exceeded by a
miter-clip join's corners, which lie up to `h sqrt(L^2 + 1)` from the vertex (the
clip line is `L h` along the bisector and a corner is up to `h` across it): by up
to `h (sqrt(L^2 + 1) - L)`. The polygon expansion's tolerance hides it; at a fine
tolerance it is visible, and a bound that must hold at any tolerance needs `L h`
replaced by `h sqrt(L^2 + 1)` for the miter-clip and arcs joins.

### What checks it

`tests/curve_eval.h` is an independent curve-winding evaluator (lines,
quadratics, cubics, rational quadratics and cubics flattened in `long double` to
`t / 64`), first checked on circles and ellipses and seen to fail with a flipped
sign of a rational term. The expansion is compared with the geometric oracle of
`tests/stroke_oracle.h` over the whole case matrix of the polygon expansion
(`tests/stroke_cases.h`): at every point the oracle decides the nonzero fill
agrees, no ring winds negatively, the outline lies in the stroke's box, and the
worst deviation measured is a recorded property (0.50 of `t` with boundary samples
`t` apart, 0.06 of `t` with samples `t / 8` apart). Flattened at the polygon
expansion's tolerance `tau`, the curves and the polygon agree within `2 tau + t`
(the triangle inequality), and the worst measured disagreement, recorded, is 0.70
`tau`, within the `2 tau` that bound would have to be for a plain doubling. The
lowering is compared with `tests/curves_vectors.h`, an exact model in decimal
arithmetic. `tools/stroke-curves-svg.c` and `tools/resvg-stroke/zoom.py` render
nine strokes three ways with a pinned external renderer at 1x, 16x and 64x: the
stroke as a stroke, the curve outline as a fill, and the polygon as a fill. At 64x
the fill's boundary is within 0.06 pixel on average of the renderer's stroke, and
the polygon, the control, within 0.3 to 1.0 pixel, so the gate is seen to fail
on the facets it exists to remove. A stroke wider than twice the radius of its
circle is not painted by that renderer, so its reference is the disc the stroke
covers.

### The revision

`GSHGEOM_STROKE_CURVES_REVISION` and `gshgeom_stroke_curves_revision()` number the
construction (1). The number is in no hash; there is no selector for another revision.

The construction is the pieces above: exact lines and arcs, Hermite offsets with the certified bound, folds as chord polygons, inflated fans, the arcs join as trapezoids, and the lowering by bisection.
